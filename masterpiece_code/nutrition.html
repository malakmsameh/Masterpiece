Small shifts, big difference

Everyday nutrition tips.

Balanced plate

Build a colorful bowl.

Aim for half vegetables, a quarter protein, and a quarter whole grains. Color usually means variety — and variety means more nutrients.

Hydration

Water first, then everything else.

Aim for around 6–8 glasses a day, more in hot weather or after exercise. Herbal tea, water-rich fruit and soups all count.

Fiber

Feed your gut kindly.

Whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fruit bring fiber that keeps you fuller for longer and supports a healthy gut.

Protein

Spread protein across the day.

Rather than one big portion at dinner, include some protein at each meal — yogurt, eggs, chicken, fish, beans or lentils.

Fats

Choose fats that love you back.

Olive oil, tahini, nuts, seeds and avocado are staples of the Mediterranean table — enjoy them in sensible portions.

Mindful eating

Slow down, sit down.

Eating without screens, chewing slowly, and enjoying the taste and company can help you notice when you're satisfied.

A longer read

A gentle guide to eating well — the Middle Eastern way.

For generations, the food traditions of the Levant, Egypt and the wider region have quietly delivered what modern nutrition science is only now catching up on: meals rich in vegetables, legumes, olive oil, herbs, whole grains and moderate portions of protein.

1. Start with vegetables and herbs.

Tabbouleh, fattoush, and simple tomato-and-cucumber salads are more than sides — they can be half the plate. Fresh parsley, mint, and cilantro add flavor and antioxidants.

2. Make legumes a habit, not a substitute.

Hummus, foul, lentils and chickpeas are gentle on the wallet, rich in fiber, and satisfying. Pair them with grains for a complete protein profile.

3. Use olive oil thoughtfully.

A drizzle of good olive oil is a pleasure. A little goes a long way — a tablespoon is roughly 119 kcal, so measuring helps when portions matter.

4. Keep protein portions moderate and varied.

Chicken, fish, eggs, yogurt and legumes rotate easily across a week. Aim for a palm-sized portion at most meals.

5. Let grains be grains.

Rice, bulgur, freekeh and whole-wheat bread are nourishing in sensible portions. Pair them with protein and fiber to slow digestion.

6. Enjoy sweets and treats, mindfully.

Baklava, knafeh and dates have a place. Enjoy them with intention and let the rest of the day's choices balance them out.

7. Sit, share, and take your time.

The social side of food — the mezze table, the shared pot — is part of what makes eating well sustainable. Food is connection as much as fuel.

These are general suggestions. Personal needs vary with age, activity, health conditions and goals. For anything specific, please consult a qualified professional.

Friendly myth-busting

Let's clear a few things up.

Myth

Carbs are the enemy.

Carbs are your body's preferred energy source. The quality and portion matter — whole grains, fruit and vegetables bring valuable nutrients.

Fact

Balance beats extremes.

Long-term wellbeing comes from consistent, enjoyable patterns — not short-term restriction.

Myth

You must eat every three hours.

Meal timing is personal. Some thrive on three meals; others prefer smaller, more frequent meals. Hunger and fullness cues are good guides.

Fact

Fiber is underrated.

Most adults in the region get less fiber than the recommended 25–30 g per day. Legumes, whole grains and vegetables are easy wins.

Myth

Snacking is always bad.

A planned, balanced snack can help stabilize energy, especially if meals are spaced out or you're active.

Fact

Hydration affects how you feel.

Even mild dehydration can affect concentration, mood and digestion. Keeping a glass or bottle nearby is a simple habit that pays off.
